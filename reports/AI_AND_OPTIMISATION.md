## 1. Architectural Principle: Deterministic Core, Optional & Accountable AI Edge

Section 10 establishes a non-negotiable design rule:
> **AI must not become an unaccountable planning authority.**

In Participatory Economics, the legitimacy of an economic plan derives from the fact that workers' and consumers' councils **propose, revise, and ratify their own activities** in light of transparent social opportunity costs (Albert & Hahnel 1991a, Ch. 4; 1991b, Ch. 9). If an opaque neural network or LLM agent replaces council deliberation or secretly manipulates indicative prices, it recreates the "coordinator class" monopoly of information that Parecon was designed to abolish (PEPE Ch. 1, pp. 15–17).

Therefore, DPEP enforces a strict **two-tier computational separation**:
1. **The Core Allocation and Clearing Engine (`Layer 1 & Layer 2`) Is 100% Deterministic, Non-AI Mathematical Programming:** Indicative price updates, Merkle-Sum supply/demand aggregation, $SB/SC$ calculations, and budget checks use transparent, reproducible fixed-point arithmetic and convex/conic operations research (OR) algorithms that contain **zero** machine learning weights or stochastic black boxes.
2. **Decision Support, Forecasting, and Accessibility Tools (`Layer 4`) Are Strictly Advisory, Local-First, and Auditable:** Statistical models, OR solvers, and optional local LLMs run at the edge (on council or household nodes) to reduce cognitive fatigue—drafting initial proposals from last year's history, translating qualitative `Lists`, or highlighting input bottlenecks—where every recommendation is accompanied by an explicit explanation and requires explicit human/council sign-off.

## 2. Comparative Evaluation of Six Computational Paradigms (Section 10)

Before evaluating specific use cases, we compare the six algorithmic families specified in Section 10:

| Paradigm | Transparency & Verifiability | Compute & Energy Cost | Determinism & Reproducibility | Primary Role in DPEP |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Deterministic Rule Algorithms** (Fixed-point price updates, MST sum, accounting identities) | **100% Inspectable & Bit-for-Bit Verifiable** (via `Wasmtime`) | Ultra-low ($\mu\text{s}$ to $\text{ms}$, $<0.001\text{ Wh}$) | **Strict** | **Mandatory Core Engine:** IFB price updates, $SB/SC$ and budget checks, Society Account clearing. |
| **2. Operations Research (OR) & Mathematical Optimisation** (LP, Conic QP, MILP, ADMM via `Clarabel.rs` / `HiGHS`) | **High:** KKT dual certificates prove optimality and shadow sensitivities | Low ($\text{ms}$ to seconds on laptop/phone) | **High** (with fixed solver tolerances & pivot rules) | **Primary Council Decision Support:** Solves local WC input-substitution problems, BJC scheduling, and late-round IFB feasible-plan synthesis. |
| **3. Classical Statistical Forecasting** (ARIMA, ETS, Hierarchical Reconciliation / MinT, Demographic Extrapolation) | **High:** Explicit confidence intervals and closed-form parameters | Very Low ($\text{ms}$ on CPU) | **Strict** | **Default Forecasting Engine:** Pre-round baseline demand projections and coarse-to-granular SKU size/color splits (DEP Ch. 8). |
| **4. Conventional Machine Learning** (Gradient Boosted Trees [LightGBM/XGBoost], Sparse Lasso, Isolation Forests) | **Medium:** Feature importance (SHAP) inspectable, but requires training data audit | Low-Medium (CPU inference in $\text{ms}$) | **High at inference** (fixed model hash) | **Secondary Analytical Support:** Weather/crop yield forecasting, multi-variable elasticity estimation, and anomaly detection. |
| **5. LLM-Based Assistants** (Small open-weight local models, e.g., 3B–8B quantized GGUF on council nodes) | **Low:** Prone to hallucination and framing bias; outputs cannot be trusted as facts | High (Seconds on GPU/NPU; significant energy if overused) | **Low** (even at `temp=0`, hardware floating-point varies) | **Strictly Bounded Accessibility & Summarization Layer:** Translating/summarizing qualitative `Lists` with mandatory citation links to raw council text. |
| **6. Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)** (Rule-based / RL autonomous bidding agents) | **Medium-Low:** Emergent strategic dynamics can oscillate or game rules | Medium | **Seed-dependent** | **Offline Stress-Testing & Simulation Only:** Used by researchers to stress-test protocol rules before deployment—**never** allowed to vote or commit on behalf of human councils! |

## 4. Economic and Institutional Failure Modes (Section 11)

Technological sophistication cannot rescue an incoherent economic mechanism or a flawed institutional design. In compliance with Section 11, we critically examine twenty potential failure modes of Participatory Economics, explicitly classifying each by **Root Origin**:
- **[ECON-MODEL]:** Intrinsic to the formal economic theory of Parecon.
- **[INST-DESIGN]:** Arising from council/federation rules and human governance dynamics.
- **[SW-ARCH]:** Arising from distributed computing, cryptography, or UI engineering.
- **[ADOPTION]:** Arising from real-world transition, culture, or political economy.

We also distinguish which questions can be answered via **Computer Simulation (`[SIM]`)** versus which require **Human-in-the-Loop Experiments or Pilot Communities (`[HUMAN]`)**.

### 4.1 Comprehensive Failure Mode Analysis Matrix

| # | Failure Mode | Root Origin | Critical Technical & Economic Analysis | protocol/Institutional Mitigation & Limits | Verification Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Non-Convergence at Scale (Lumpy Capacities & Non-Convexities)** | **[ECON-MODEL]** | As proven in our audit of `pequod-cljs` (`research/sources/hahnel_2021_dep_analysis.md`), published simulations assumed smooth Cobb-Douglas functions with a convex effort-disutility barrier. Real industries have fixed setup costs, minimum efficient scales (discrete plant openings/shutdowns), and complementarity (Leontief recipes), which cause pure price tâtonnement to oscillate between corner solutions. | Replace pure unregularized price tâtonnement with **Proximal / Augmented-Lagrangian (ADMM) Damping** (trust-region bounds on round-to-round quantity jumps, PEPE Ch. 4 p. 70) + **Industry Federation capacity coordination** for discrete plant additions/closures. | **[SIM]:** Test 10,000+ councils with piecewise-linear Leontief & mixed-integer setup costs. |
| **2. Computational Complexity of High-Dimensional Input-Output Clearing** | **[ECON-MODEL]** + **[SW-ARCH]** | An economy has tens of millions of specific SKUs ("size 6 purple women's shoe," DEP Ch. 8 p. 163). Running iterative council bargaining over $10^7$ SKUs would diverge or take months. | **Two-Tier Product Taxonomy (DEP Ch. 8; Sandström 2025):** Annual iterative planning runs over $5,000\text{–}50,000$ coarse resource-equivalent product classes; granular SKU attributes are disaggregated statistically and cleared via continuous intra-year inventory buffers. | **[SIM]:** Benchmark coarse-to-fine disaggregation error and buffer stock sizing. |
| **3. Communication & Round-Trip Latency Complexity** | **[INST-DESIGN]** + **[SW-ARCH]** | Even if CPU time per round is 5 seconds, if each round requires synchronous in-person meetings of 400,000 councils, 15 rounds would take 15+ weeks of exhausting meetings. | **Decouple Automated Sub-Iterations from Human Milestone Rounds:** Councils set local preference/recipe parameters and trust-region bounds in Round 1, run 3–5 rapid automated solver sub-iterations to clear intermediate input prices, and reconvene humans only at 3–4 milestone deliberation rounds! | **[SIM]** + **[HUMAN]** (test human deliberation time per milestone round). |
| **4. Incentive Compatibility & Strategic Misreporting** | **[ECON-MODEL]** | By Hurwicz's (1972) impossibility theorem, no decentralized mechanism is dominant-strategy incentive-compatible, Pareto-optimal, and budget-balanced in general environments. A WC might understate its technical productivity ($a_j$) to enjoy an easier work pace while still meeting $SB_j/SC_j = 1$. | 1. **Public Books of Blueprints (PEPE Ch. 5 p. 84)** & inter-firm $SB/SC$ benchmarking within each Industry Federation expose productivity outliers. 2. **Capping average effort ratings at $SB_j/SC_j$** removes profit-hoarding incentives. However, tacit local knowledge hiding cannot be 100% eliminated mathematically. | **[SIM]** (game-theoretic agent simulations) + **[HUMAN]** (behavioral lab experiments). |
| **5. Free-Riding on Collective / Public Goods** | **[ECON-MODEL]** | Under standard Lindahl pricing, individuals might understate demand for public goods if charged according to reported willingness-to-pay. | Parecon charges every member of Consumer Federation $f$ an **equal (or formulaic) per-capita share** of ratified collective consumption $y_f^{\text{pub}}$ (PEPE Ch. 3 p. 43; DEP Ch. 7 p. 134). Because an individual's cost share is invariant to their vote, voting one's true preference is incentive-compatible under median-voter / pivot conditions. | **[HUMAN]** (public-goods voting experiments). |
| **6. Information Asymmetries & Tacit Knowledge (Hayek Problem)** | **[ECON-MODEL]** + **[INST-DESIGN]** | Hayek (1945) argued that tacit, local knowledge cannot be transmitted to a central planner. However, **new product innovation** requires R&D task forces and Industry Federation startup accreditation so tacit entrepreneurial ideas are not blocked by conservative incumbents. | **[HUMAN]** + institutional design of R&D / startup councils. |
| **7. Acute Scarcity and Non-Price Rationing** | **[ECON-MODEL]** | When a critical necessity (e.g., insulin, baby formula, heating fuel during a freeze) is in acute shortage, letting its indicative price skyrocket violates equity (Maxim 4). | **Dual-Regime Allocation Rule:** Essential baseline quantities of necessities are placed on the **Universal Free / Rationed Need List (Maxim 4, PEPE Ch. 3 p. 47)** allocated by medical/demographic need, while only above-baseline discretionary quantities face indicative price clearing. | **[SIM]** + **[HUMAN]**. |
| **8. Intra-Year Preference Shifts ("July vs. January" Problem)** | **[ECON-MODEL]** + **[SW-ARCH]** | Consumers cannot predict in December everything they will want to eat, wear, or read next August (Schweickart 1993). | 1. | **9. | **10. Emergency Resource Allocation (Disasters / Pandemics)** | **[INST-DESIGN]** | Iterative multi-week deliberation is too slow during an acute wildfire, earthquake, or epidemic; conversely, unchecked emergency authority risks permanent centralist capture (Paris Commune May 1871). | **Constitutionally Bounded Pre-Authorized Emergency Playbooks:** Pre-ratified priority allocation tiers activate upon multi-council threshold trigger, with a **hard 14-day TTL** and mandatory post-incident *euthynai* audit. | **[HUMAN]** + protocol state-machine verification. |
| **11. | **12. | **13. | **14. Ecological Constraints vs. Growth Bias** | **[ECON-MODEL]** | If consumer and worker councils continually propose higher consumption and output without accounting for throughput limits, aggregate material footprint grows unsustainably (O'Neill 2024). | Bind natural resource supplies $r^*$ and sink capacities $E_z^{\max}$ to **non-increasing biophysical caps** (degrowth / steady-state material throughput constraints) so rising shadow prices $p^{\text{res}}, p^{\text{pol}}$ automatically steer proposals toward low-material, high-care/cultural activities and shorter workweeks. | **[SIM]:** Multi-decade steady-state biophysical cap simulations. |
| **15. Informal Hierarchies ("Tyranny of Structurelessness")** | **[INST-DESIGN]** | Even with formal Balanced Job Complexes, articulate, confident, or long-tenured members can dominate council debates (Freeman 1972; Occupy 2011). | Combine **BJCs** (PEPE Ch. 2) with **Athenian Sortition + Rotation** for all facilitation, agenda-setting, and audit committees, plus structured asynchronous proposal amendment tools so quiet/deliberative members have equal voice. | **[HUMAN]** (cannot be tested in pure numerical simulation). |
| **16. Coordination Fatigue & Participation Costs** | **[INST-DESIGN]** + **[SW-ARCH]** | If participatory economics feels like "an endless series of evening meetings and spreadsheet homework" (Oscar Wilde / Schweickart critique), citizens will stop participating and delegate everything to a new coordinator class. | **Strict Participation Time Budget (< 2 hours/month for household consumption planning; workplace governance inside paid BJC work hours):** Local deterministic drafting assistants handle 95% of routine re-pricing adjustments automatically within user-set bounds, surfacing only meaningful value choices. | **[HUMAN]:** Measure task completion time and cognitive load (NASA-TLX) in PoC user tests. |
| **17. | **18. Federation & IFB Governance Capture** | **[INST-DESIGN]** + **[SW-ARCH]** | IFB software maintainers or federation delegates could subtly tweak price-update parameters or gatekeep council accreditation (Kuch 2024). | 1. IFB rules are **open-source deterministic WASM modules** ratified by federation vote and verified by every node. 2. Federation delegates hold **short-lived, recallable `PQ-UCAN` capabilities**. 3. | **19. Unequal Digital Access & Hardware Divide** | **[SW-ARCH]** + **[ADOPTION]** | Elderly, disabled, rural, or low-income participants may lack modern devices or continuous broadband. | **Offline-First Multi-Modal Access:** Support low-end Android phones (`Role-LP`), shared neighborhood terminals / Raspberry Pi kiosks (`Role-CC`), paper-ballot optical scan bridges at neighborhood meetings, and screen-reader/voice interfaces. | **[HUMAN]:** Accessibility & offline field testing. |

Transition & Hostile External Environment** | **[ADOPTION]** | A fledgling participatory economy network operating inside or alongside capitalist markets faces capital flight, supply-chain embargoes, and legal/state hostility (Spain 1936). | Design the protocol to be useful **incrementally today** for federated worker cooperatives, solidarity-economy networks, community land trusts, and municipal participatory budgeting ("Experiment 2: Developing a Parallel Economy," PEPE Ch. 6 pp. 129–130). | **[ADOPTION]:** Real-world cooperative federation pilots. |
