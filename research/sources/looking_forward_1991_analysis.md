## 2. Substantive Institutional and Human-Facing Requirements Extracted

### A. Workplace Organization and Balanced Job Complexes (Chs. 1, 2, 7)
1. **Multi-Tiered Workplace Councils:**
   - Small workplaces (e.g., Northstart Press, ~30 workers) operate through a full assembly and functional work teams (editorial, production, finance/promotion, computer maintenance).
   - Large workplaces (e.g., Jesse Owens Airport, thousands of workers) use nested work teams, division councils (baggage, maintenance, air traffic control, scheduling, passenger services), and a workplace-wide council and rotating administrative committee.
2. **Task Recomposition into Balanced Job Complexes (BJCs):**
   - Rejects the corporate division of labor where 20% monopolize empowering conceptual/managerial tasks and 80% perform rote execution.
   - Every worker's job complex combines 3–6 distinct responsibilities spanning conceptual, administrative, and manual/rote tasks, calibrated to the workplace average for empowerment and desirability.
   - Across-workplace balancing: Workers in intrinsically less empowering or more arduous workplaces receive scheduled work rotations in higher-empowerment workplaces or community/federation boards, coordinated via Employment Facilitation Boards (EFBs) and Job Complex Committees.
3. **Proportional Decision-Making (Self-Management):**
   - Work team decisions affecting only how that team executes its tasks are made autonomously by the team (consensus or majority).
   - Decisions affecting overall plant output or schedules require workplace council approval (one person, one vote).
   - Specialized technical analyses are prepared by trained workers within their BJC roles, but final policy choices rest with affected council members.

### B. Consumption Councils, Collective Goods, and Personal Privacy (Chs. 3, 4, 8)
1. **Three Guiding Principles of Consumption (Ch. 3, `lf3_2.htm`):**
   - *Principle 1:* Collective consumption decisions are reached collectively and judged by all affected councils.
   - *Principle 2:* Individual budget allowances depend on work effort/sacrifice histories and approved special needs, subject to collective oversight for equity.
   - *Principle 3:* Individual consumption requests are open to collective discussion/criticism, **with specific institutional guarantees preserving individual freedom and privacy** (anonymized personal requests within budget cannot be vetoed).
2. **Sequencing Collective Before Individual Consumption (Ch. 8, `lf8_1.htm`):**
   - National, state, county/city, and ward collective consumption projects (parks, transit, libraries, health facilities, cultural centers) are proposed, debated, and voted on *first* (or during initial rounds), using interactive computer simulations showing how each collective option affects per-capita private consumption budgets (including multi-year county borrowing/amortization).
   - Households and individuals then formulate private consumption requests knowing both the collective goods that will be available and the remaining per-capita private budget.
3. **Coarse Product Categorization vs. Granular Fulfillment (Ch. 6, `lf6_2.htm`; Ch. 8):**
   - Consumers do **not** plan exact SKUs (size, color, style) during annual planning.
   - Planning operates over coarse product categories (e.g., "women's shoes," "bicycles," "cotton shirts") grouped by interchangeable resource/labor inputs.
   - Specific styles, sizes, and colors are predicted statistically from demographic data and historical draw-downs, and selected by individuals during the year at distribution centers/outlets, triggering real-time inventory signals to Updating Facilitation Boards (UFBs) and industry federations.

### C. Taxonomy of Facilitation Boards (Ch. 6, `lf6_1.htm`; Ch. 9, `lf9_2.htm`)
*Looking Forward* explicitly defines **six distinct types of facilitation boards** (whereas *PEPE* Ch. 5 collapses them into a single abstract Planning Bureau `PB`):
1. **Iteration Facilitation Boards (IFBs):** Aggregate supply and demand, compute percentage imbalances, update indicative prices, publish projections of average income and $SB/SC$ ratios, and (optionally in late rounds) synthesize ~5 feasible macro-plan options for council voting.
2. **Consumption Facilitation Boards (CFBs):** Assist neighborhood, ward, county, and regional consumer councils in preparing and evaluating collective consumption proposals and cost-sharing implications.
3. **Production Facilitation Boards (PFBs):** Assist worker councils and industry/regional federations in evaluating production externalities, economies of scale, and technology adoption.
4. **Employment Facilitation Boards (EFBs):** Assist workers transferring between councils when industries expand or contract, and coordinate cross-workplace job complex balancing.
5. **Household Facilitation Boards (HFBs):** Assist individuals and families in finding housing and membership in living units, co-housing communities, and neighborhoods.
6. **Updating Facilitation Boards (UFBs):** Coordinate intra-year plan modifications when consumer preferences shift or unexpected supply disruptions occur, finding the least disruptive adjustments across councils.

### D. Safeguards Against Technocratic Capture and Sabotage (Ch. 9, `lf9_2.htm`; Ch. 10, `lf10_1.htm`)
- **Four Institutional Safeguards on IFBs (`lf9_2.htm`):**
  1. IFB workers have balanced job complexes and receive average effort-rated income, eliminating material incentives to skew prices.
  2. Society (not IFB workers) decides whether IFB price updates follow a strictly deterministic mathematical rule or allow bounded human discretion.
  3. All IFB data banks, algorithms, and discretionary adjustments are 100% open to public inspection and audit by any council or citizen.
  4. IFBs have **zero authority** to approve, veto, or mandate any council's proposal; their outputs are purely informational guidelines, and councils can reject late-round IFB macro-plan bundles or demand additional options.
- **Protection Against Sabotage (`lf9_2.htm`):** Explicitly notes that early implementations face risks of deliberate sabotage or manipulated submissions, requiring verification and integrity safeguards.
