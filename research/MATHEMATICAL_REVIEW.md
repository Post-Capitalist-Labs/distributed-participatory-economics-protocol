## 1. Executive Summary of Mathematical Findings

1. **Limits of Unconditional Tâtonnement Convergence (`R-ECON-01`, `R-ECON-02`):** An earlier draft of Section 4.4.3 stated that adding a proximal penalty and multiplicative trust regions to the Hahnel-Weisdorf-Szczepanczyk price rule ("Algorithm 3") *"guarantees convergence"* under Leontief complementarities, endogenous income feedback, and non-convexities, while Chapter 10 simultaneously listed those exact properties as unresolved open questions (`P1`, `P2`, `P4`). All unconditional convergence claims are removed: **no iterative price mechanism guarantees global convergence to a feasible clearing plan across general non-convex or strong-income-effect economies.**
2. **Audit of the `pequod-cljs` "Toothache" Concavity Analysis (`R-ECON-06`):** An earlier draft suggested that `pequod-cljs`'s increasing-returns-to-scale ("toothache") experiment remained strictly concave because the effort disutility exponent $k \in [3, 4]$ kept $\sum_m b_m + \frac{c}{k} < 1$. Direct audit of all 150,000 Worker Council records across the 70 `dep1ex*.clj` files in `msszczep/pequod-cljs` (`8bd46b5c233f0e48c767ad6d5069b9437a67c38b`) proves the opposite: while baseline councils satisfy $\sum_m b_m + \frac{c}{k} \in [0.7706, 0.8756] < 1$, applying `create-toothache` ($+0.1$ to each of the 3 to 8 input exponents) yields $\sum_m b_m + \frac{c}{k} \in [1.0712, 1.6652] > 1$ for **100% of selected councils (150,000 / 150,000)**. When $\sum_m b_m + \frac{c}{k} > 1$, the worker council's unconstrained objective function is **unbounded above ($\sup \Pi_j = +\infty$)** along the ray $x_m = t, e = t^{1/k}$, and the closed-form stationary point (`solution-1` to `solution-8`) is a **saddle point / local minimum along the scaling ray** where supply responds *perversely* (inversely) to output price! Thus, our core conclusion—that `pequod-cljs` did not validly test non-convex increasing returns—is **strengthened**, while the mathematical explanation is placed on an exact footing.
3. **Separation of Heuristic Proximal Tâtonnement from True Augmented Lagrangian / ADMM (`R-ECON-02`):** Combining a local proximal term $\frac{\mu}{2}\|\pi_j - \pi_j^{(t-1)}\|^2$ with the heuristic sign-based multiplicative price rule (Eq. 4.15) is **Proximal Damped Tâtonnement**, *not* the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM). True ADMM requires coupling the global market-clearing residual $ED^{(t)} = D^{(t)} + SL^{(t)} - S^{(t)}$ inside each council's objective via a quadratic penalty $\frac{\rho_{\text{pen}}}{2}\|D + SL - S\|_2^2$ (linearized via Jacobi/proximal splitting) and updating dual prices linearly by $P^{(t+1)} = [P^{(t)} + \rho_{\text{pen}} ED^{(t)}]_+$.
4. **Closure of the Macro-Accounting Identity and Numéraire Normalisation (`R-ECON-05`, `R-ECON-07`):** We fix the price-level scale indeterminacy under endogenous budgets by introducing an explicit **Numéraire Normalisation Invariant** ($w_{\text{base}} \equiv 1.0\text{ accounting unit / standard effort-hour}$, or a fixed consumer basket index $\sum_{g} \omega_g P_g \equiv 1$), and we close the Society Account equation by dividing the worker consumption pool by **effective effort-weighted hours** $H^{\text{eff}} = \sum_{u} h_u \epsilon_u$ and explicitly reconciling CAP pollution fees $\sum_j p_z^{\text{pol}} e_{j,z}$ against CAP compensation payouts $p_z^{\text{pol}} E_z$ and net household savings/borrowing $\sum_u \Delta S_u = 0$.

### 2.2 Proof of Objective Unboundedness and Perverse Supply in `create-toothache` (`R-ECON-06`)

In `pequod-cljs` (`wcs.clj` lines 31–70; Szczepanczyk 2023, Eqs. 2–6), each Worker Council $WC_j$ with $n \in \{3, \dots, 8\}$ inputs $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and effort $e > 0$ has production function and objective:
$$y(x, e) = a e^c \prod_{m=1}^n x_m^{b_m}, \qquad \Pi(x, e) = \lambda a e^c \prod_{m=1}^n x_m^{b_m} - \sum_{m=1}^n p_m x_m - s e^k$$
where $a \in [4, 6]$, $c \in [0.05, 0.10]$, $k \in [3, 4]$ (`:du`), $s = 1$, and baseline exponents are drawn from $b_m \in \left[\frac{0.75}{n}, \frac{0.85}{n}\right]$ so that $\sum_{m=1}^n b_m \in [0.75, 0.85]$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Concavity Condition and Unboundedness of `pequod-cljs` Worker Council Problem):**
1. *Strict Concavity Condition:* Substituting the optimal effort $e^*(x) = \left(\frac{\lambda a c}{s k} \prod_{m=1}^n x_m^{b_m}\right)^{\frac{1}{k - c}}$ into $\Pi(x, e)$ yields a reduced objective proportional to $\left(\prod_{m=1}^n x_m^{b_m}\right)^{\frac{k}{k - c}} - \sum_{m=1}^n p_m x_m$. This reduced objective is strictly concave on $\mathbb{R}_{++}^n$ if and only if the sum of reduced exponents is less than 1:
   $$\frac{k}{k - c} \sum_{m=1}^n b_m < 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \sum_{m=1}^n b_m + \frac{c}{k} < 1$$
2. *Behaviour Under `create-toothache`:* In `wcs.clj` (`create-toothache`), each of the $n \in \{3, \dots, 8\}$ exponents is increased by $+0.1$, so $\sum_{m=1}^n b_m^{\text{tooth}} = \sum_{m=1}^n b_m + 0.1 n \ge 0.75 + 0.30 = 1.05 > 1$. Across all 150,000 WC records in `dep1ex01.clj`–`dep1ex70.clj` audited by `research/analysis/verify_pequod_datasets.py`:
   $$\text{Baseline: } \sum_{m=1}^n b_m + \frac{c}{k} \in [0.7706, 0.8756] < 1 \quad (\text{100\% strictly concave})$$
   $$\text{Toothache: } \sum_{m=1}^n b_m^{\text{tooth}} + \frac{c}{k} \in [1.0712, 1.6652] > 1 \quad (\text{100\% non-concave / unbounded!})$$
3. *Ray to $+\infty$ and Perverse Comparative Statics at the Stationary Point:* Along the ray $x_m(t) = t$ and $e(t) = t^{1/k}$ as $t \to \infty$, cost grows linearly as $\left(\sum_{m=1}^n p_m + s\right) t$, whereas revenue grows super-linearly as $\lambda a t^{\sum b_m + c/k}$. Because $\sum b_m + c/k > 1$, $\lim_{t \to \infty} \Pi(x(t), e(t)) = +\infty$. In `solution-1..8` (`csvgen.clj` lines 209–376), the log-output formula has denominator:
   $$D = c - k + k \sum_{m=1}^n b_m = k \left(\sum_{m=1}^n b_m + \frac{c}{k} - 1\right)$$
   and numerator term $-\left(c + k \sum_{m=1}^n b_m\right) \ln \lambda$. Therefore, the elasticity of the closed-form stationary output $y^{\text{FOC}}$ with respect to output price $\lambda$ is:
   $$\frac{\partial \ln y^{\text{FOC}}}{\partial \ln \lambda} = \frac{-\left(c + k \sum_{m=1}^n b_m\right)}{k \left(\sum_{m=1}^n b_m + \frac{c}{k} - 1\right)}$$
   - When $\sum b_m + \frac{c}{k} < 1$ (baseline), the denominator is negative and the numerator is negative, so $\frac{\partial \ln y^{\text{FOC}}}{\partial \ln \lambda} > 0$ (normal upward-sloping supply).
   - When $\sum b_m + \frac{c}{k} > 1$ (`toothache`), the denominator becomes **positive**, so $\frac{\partial \ln y^{\text{FOC}}}{\partial \ln \lambda} < 0$! That is, the closed-form formula in `csvgen.clj` evaluates a **saddle point (minimum along the scale ray)** where a toothache council *reduces* output when its output price rises and *increases* output when its output price falls! The only reason the overall economy still converged in `dep2ex01..50` is that the remaining 80% of normal councils overwhelmed the 20% perverse-supply saddle-point councils.

## 3. Critical Reassessment of Iterative Convergence (`R-ECON-01` to `R-ECON-05`, `R-ECON-07` to `R-ECON-11`)

### 3.1 Why General Tâtonnement Convergence Cannot Be Guaranteed (`R-ECON-03`: Scarf's Counterexample)
By the **Sonnenschein–Mantel–Debreu (SMD) theorem** (Sonnenschein 1973; Mantel 1974; Debreu 1974), aggregate excess demand functions in general exchange economies inherit only continuity, homogeneity of degree zero, and Walras's Law—not gross substitutability or uniqueness/stability of equilibrium. **Herbert Scarf (1960)** demonstrated that even with 3 goods and 3 strictly quasi-concave Leontief consumers, price tâtonnement can enter a permanent limit cycle from any non-equilibrium initial price vector.

In `research/analysis/verify_economics.py` (`test_r_econ_03_scarf`), we tested the exact Hahnel-Weisdorf-Szczepanczyk price rule (Eqs. 4.14–4.15) on Scarf's 3-good cyclic Leontief economy ($S = (1,1,1)$, $P^{(0)} = (1.3, 1.0, 0.8)$) with and without multiplicative trust regions $|\Delta y_{i,g}| \le \beta^{(t)} y_{i,g}^{(t-1)}$ over $3,000$ rounds:
- **No damping / $\beta = 0.25$ / $\beta = 0.10$ / $\beta = 0.02$:** Minimum $\max_i \rho_i^{(t)}$ achieved over 3,000 rounds is **`0.105444` ($\approx 10.5\%$)**—never reaching the 5% threshold.
- **Decaying trust region ($\beta^{(t)} = 0.25 \cdot 0.99^t$):** Proposals freeze as $\beta^{(t)} \to 0$ while excess demand remains stuck at **`56.6%` (`final_max_rho = 0.565951`)**.
- **Mathematical Lesson:** A decaying trust region $\sum_t \beta^{(t)} < \infty$ forces $\|y^{(t)} - y^{(t-1)}\| \to 0$ (**stalling**), which must never be confused with **market-clearing convergence** ($\|ED^{(t)}\| \to 0$).

### 3.2 Non-Convexities, Fixed Setup Costs, and the Shapley–Folkman Theorem (`R-ECON-04`)
When a single indivisible plant has a minimum efficient scale or fixed start-up threshold—e.g., $S(p) = 100$ if $p \ge 10$ and $0$ if $p < 10$, facing continuous demand $D(p) = 70 - p$—**no market-clearing price exists**:
- For $p < 10$, $S(p) = 0$ and $D(p) > 60$ ($\rho = 2.0$, 200% shortage).
- For $p \ge 10$, $S(p) = 100$ and $D(p) \le 60$ ($\rho \ge \frac{2(40)}{160} = 0.50$, $\ge 50\%$ surplus).
Our simulation (`verify_economics.py`, `test_r_econ_04_nonconvex_plant`) confirms that over 500 rounds starting at $p = 8$, the imbalance chatters across $p = 10$ and never falls below **`0.5037`**.

However, by the **Shapley–Folkman–Starr theorem** (Starr 1969), the Minkowski sum of $K$ non-convex production sets $\sum_{j=1}^K \mathcal{T}_j$ has a non-convexity radius bounded by $O(\max_j \text{diam}(\text{conv}(\mathcal{T}_j) \setminus \mathcal{T}_j))$, which shrinks relative to total output as $1/K$. When the same 100-unit capacity is distributed across $K$ smaller worker councils with staggered cost thresholds in $[8.5, 11.5]$, our simulation shows the minimum imbalance drops from `0.5037` ($K=1$) to **`0.0040` ($0.4\%$, $K=20$)** and **`0.0048` ($K=100$)**.
- **Implication for Participatory Planning:** Iterative price coordination can clear aggregates across industries composed of *many small/medium worker councils*, but **cannot** clear lumpy, indivisible facilities (e.g., a single steel mill, semiconductor fab, or rail trunk line) via autonomous local price responses alone; such indivisible facilities require explicit **Industry Federation discrete capacity commitment** (mixed-integer coordination) prior to continuous price clearing.

### 3.3 Endogenous Income Feedback and the Numéraire Requirement (`R-ECON-05`)
In `pequod-cljs`, consumer budgets $I_h = 5,000$ were held constant in nominal units, anchoring the absolute price level. When nominal consumer budgets are instead updated endogenously from nominal GDP ($Y^{\text{GDP}}(P^{(t)})$) via Eq. 4.16, both supply and demand become **homogeneous of degree zero** in $(P, w_{\text{base}})$: multiplying all prices $P$ and $w_{\text{base}}$ by any scalar $\lambda > 0$ leaves all real quantities unchanged!
- Our reproduction (`verify_economics.py`, `test_r_econ_05_numeraire`) confirms that while the relative price $P_1 / P_2$ converges to $\approx 0.522$ in both runs, starting at $P^{(0)} = (700, 700)$ ends at $P_1 \approx 588.16$ (or $597$ under synchronous delta lag), whereas starting at $P^{(0)} = (1400, 1400)$ ends at $P_1 \approx 1054.70$ (or $977$).
- **Mathematical Fix:** Every endogenous-income planning model must fix an explicit **Numéraire Invariant**: either (a) fix the base hourly effort wage as the unit of account ($w_{\text{base}} \equiv 1.0\text{ accounting credit / standard effort-hour}$, so all indicative prices $P_i$ are expressed directly in standard effort-hour equivalents!), or (b) normalize the price vector after each round so that the Laspeyres/basket-weighted price index satisfies $\sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}^{\text{priv}}} \omega_g P_g^{(t)} \equiv P_{\text{index}}^0$. Fixing $w_{\text{base}} \equiv 1.0$ (or normalising the price index) eliminates nominal scale drift completely and removes the arbitrary relaxation parameter $\alpha_w$.

### 3.4 Exact Closure of the Macro-Accounting Identities (`R-ECON-07`)
In an earlier draft, Equation 4.6 defined $w_{\text{base}}(P) = Y^{\text{work}}(P) / H^{\text{tot}}$ using unweighted hours $H^{\text{tot}} = \sum_u h_u$, while Equation 4.8 paid each worker $w_{\text{base}}(P) h_u \epsilon_u$. As `R-ECON-07` correctly notes, if the hours-weighted mean effort rating across the economy deviates from $1.0$, $\sum_u w_{\text{base}} h_u \epsilon_u \ne Y^{\text{work}}(P)$! Furthermore, CAP compensation and net household borrowing must be explicitly reconciled.

**Closed Macro-Accounting System (Equations 4.4–4.8):**
1. **Total Social Value of Net Final Output (GDP):**
   $$Y^{\text{GDP}}(P) = p^{\text{priv}} \cdot D^{\text{priv}} + p^{\text{pub}} \cdot D^{\text{pub}} + p^{\text{cap,new}} \cdot D^{\text{inv}}$$
2. **CAP Pollution Account Balance (Separate from GDP or Explicitly Netted):**
   Each Worker Council $WC_j$ is charged $p^{\text{pol}} \cdot e_j$ for its emissions, which is credited to the respective CAP accounts. At equilibrium where $\sum_{j=1}^J e_{j,z} = E_z$, total polluter fees collected equal total CAP victim compensation credits distributed:
   $$Y^{\text{CAP}}(P) = \sum_{z \in \mathcal{Z}} p_z^{\text{pol}} E_z = \sum_{z \in \mathcal{Z}} p_z^{\text{pol}} \sum_{j=1}^J e_{j,z} \quad (\text{exact balance at } ED_z^{\text{pol}} = 0)$$
3. **Net Household Saving/Borrowing Clearing Condition:**
   Across the society in any given year, net household borrowing and saving must either balance in aggregate ($\sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta S_u = 0$) or any net aggregate household saving/dissaving $\Delta S^{\text{agg}} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta S_u$ is deducted directly from the aggregate investment share $\sigma_{\text{inv}}$.
4. **Exact Effort-Weighted Base Wage Rate ($w_{\text{base}}$):**
   Let $H^{\text{eff}} = \sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}^{\text{work}}} h_u \epsilon_u$ be the **total effective effort-weighted work hours**. To ensure that total consumption allowances (work income + social allowances + CAP credits + net borrowing) equal total planned private and public consumption $(1 - \sigma_{\text{inv}}) Y^{\text{GDP}}(P)$, the work-income pool and base wage per effort-weighted hour are:
   $$Y^{\text{work}}(P) = (1 - \sigma_{\text{inv}}) Y^{\text{GDP}}(P) - Y^{\text{allow}} - Y^{\text{CAP}}(P) - \Delta S^{\text{agg}}, \qquad w_{\text{base}}(P) = \frac{Y^{\text{work}}(P)}{\sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}^{\text{work}}} h_u \epsilon_u}$$
   Summing $B_u(P)$ over all $u \in \mathcal{I}$ now satisfies **exact Walras's Law / Society Account conservation**:
   $$\sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}} B_u(P) = w_{\text{base}}(P) \sum_{u \in \mathcal{I}^{\text{work}}} h_u \epsilon_u + Y^{\text{allow}} + Y^{\text{CAP}}(P) + \Delta S^{\text{agg}} \equiv (1 - \sigma_{\text{inv}}) Y^{\text{GDP}}(P)$$

### 3.5 One-Sided Feasibility Conditions and Hard Ecological Constraints (`R-ECON-10`)
A two-sided relative imbalance check $\rho_i^{(t)} = \frac{2|S_i^{(t)} - D_i^{(t)}|}{S_i^{(t)} + D_i^{(t)}} \le \tau$ permits demand $D_i^{(t)}$ to **exceed** supply $S_i^{(t)}$ by up to $\frac{1 + \tau/2}{1 - \tau/2} - 1 \approx \tau$ (e.g., 5.1% physical shortage or 5.1% breach of an ecological cap!). Moreover, an exterior penalty $\gamma_{\text{eco}} \max(0, D_z/E_z^{\max} - 1)$ only activates *after* $D_z > E_z^{\max}$, so its stationary point always lies strictly *outside* $E_z^{\max}$.

**Corrected Feasibility & Interior Barrier Specification:**
1. **One-Sided Physical & Ecological Feasibility Criterion:** A plan is physically and ecologically feasible at tolerance $\tau$ if and only if:
   - For all produced goods $g \in \mathcal{G}^{\text{priv}} \cup \mathcal{G}^{\text{pub}} \cup \mathcal{G}^{\text{int}}$: **no physical shortage** ($D_g^{(t)} \le S_g^{(t)}$) and bounded surplus ($\frac{S_g^{(t)} - D_g^{(t)}}{S_g^{(t)}} \le \tau$). (Equivalently, if planned slack $SL_g = \eta_g S_g$ with $\eta_g \ge \tau/2$ is included in target demand, $D_g^{(t)}$ never exceeds gross physical output $S_g^{(t)}$.)
   - For all natural resources $r \in \mathcal{R}$ and pollutants $z \in \mathcal{Z}$: **strict one-sided cap compliance** $D_r^{\text{res}} \le r_r^*$ and $D_z^{\text{pol}} \le \min(E_z, E_z^{\max})$.
2. **Interior/Augmented-Lagrangian Update for Hard Caps:** Instead of an exterior penalty that activates only when $D_z > E_z^{\max}$, update the dual shadow price $p_z^{\text{pol}}$ against a tightened safety margin $(1 - \nu) E_z^{\max}$ (or via the exact Augmented-Lagrangian dual ascent $p_z^{(t+1)} = \max\big(0, p_z^{(t)} + \rho_{\text{pen}}(D_z^{(t)} - E_z^{\max})\big)$) where stopping is forbidden whenever $D_z^{(t)} > E_z^{\max}$.
3. **Infeasibility Detection (Conflicting Minimum Needs vs. Ecological Caps):** If citizens' baseline subsistence needs $D^{\min}$ require resource/emission inputs $R(I - A)^{-1} D^{\min} > r^*$ under all known technologies $\mathcal{T}_j$, the primal feasible set is **empty**; any dual/price algorithm will send $p^{\text{res}} \to \infty$, which the protocol must detect within $T_{\max}$ rounds and flag as a **Structural Biophysical Infeasibility** requiring technological/investment or cap/need renegotiation.

## 4. Comparative Evaluation of Nine Coordination & Optimisation Methods (Section 4.3)
