# Participatory Economics Requirements Analysis

**Document Purpose:** This document extracts and classifies the institutional, economic, informational, and computational requirements of Participatory Economics (Parecon) from the primary and secondary literature prior to selecting any distributed systems technology.

In strict compliance with Section 2 of the Research Brief, every requirement is classified into one of four categories:
1. **[ORIGINAL-1991]:** Explicitly present in Michael Albert and Robin Hahnel's 1991 foundational texts (*The Political Economy of Participatory Economics* [PEPE] and *Looking Forward* [LF]).
2. **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]:** Formalizations, refinements, or operational interpretations introduced in subsequent Parecon literature (Albert 2003 *Parecon: Life After Capitalism* [PLAC], Hahnel 2021 *Democratic Economic Planning* [DEP], Hahnel 2022, Sandström 2020/2023 *Anarchist Accounting*, Szczepanczyk 2023 `pequod-cljs`, Participatory Economy Project 2023).
3. **[TECH-EXTENSION]:** Proposed technological extensions required to execute the model over a modern, decentralized, fault-tolerant, privacy-preserving computational protocol.
4. **[UNRESOLVED]:** Open theoretical, economic, or institutional questions that are not settled in the existing literature and require empirical, simulation, or human-factors research.

## 1. Normative & Institutional Axioms

| ID | Requirement Statement | Classification | Source Citation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **REQ-NORM-01** | **Social Ownership of the Productive Commons:** Private ownership of natural resources and produced means of production is abolished. Productive assets belong jointly to society; worker councils acquire temporary *user rights* during planning and pay indicative opportunity-cost rental charges. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 1 (pp. 17–20), Ch. 5 (p. 85); DEP Ch. 6 (pp. 98–102), Ch. 12 (pp. 244–245); Sandström (2020, Ch. 3). |
| **REQ-NORM-02** | **Collective Economic Self-Management:** Decision-making input must be structured in proportion to the degree that actors are affected by an economic choice—neither laissez-faire unilateralism nor uniform national majority rule over local workplace/consumption matters, nor a single rigid voting rule (flexibly selecting between consensus/veto for small-team hiring and majority/supermajority with minority delay for capital investments). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 1 (pp. 8, 16), Ch. 2 (p. 24); LF Ch. 1; PLAC Ch. 5 ("Consensus?"); DEP Ch. 1 (pp. 14–16). |
| **REQ-NORM-03** | **Remuneration for Effort and Sacrifice Tempered by Need:** Consumption allowances for able-bodied adults are proportional to duration, intensity, and onerousness of socially valued work (Maxim 3), supplemented by universal free public goods (e.g., healthcare, education) and council-approved special-need allowances (Maxim 4). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 1 (pp. 8, 21), Ch. 3 (pp. 44–55), Ch. 4 (pp. 60–61); LF Ch. 3; DEP Ch. 1 (pp. 16–22), Ch. 6 (pp. 108–122). |
| **REQ-NORM-04** | **Balanced Job Complexes (BJCs):** Work tasks are partitioned by empowerment and desirability effects and combined into job complexes balanced within each workplace and across workplaces via rotation assignments overseen by internal and economy-wide Job Complex Committees. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 2 (pp. 25–30), Ch. 5 (pp. 82–83); LF Chs. 1–2. |
| **REQ-NORM-05** | **No Tradable Currencies, Capital Markets, or Wage Labor:** Consumption allowances are non-transferable accounting credits between individuals and the social clearing system; there is no secondary market exchange between individuals or firms, no private equity, and no interest-bearing private lending. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 1 (pp. 12–20), Ch. 3 (pp. 48–49); Sandström (2020, Chs. 2–3). |
| **REQ-NORM-06** | **Protection Against Endogenous Preference Warping:** Allocation prices must reflect true social costs and benefits (including public goods and ecological externalities) without systematic bias, preventing snowballing divergence from Pareto optimality. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 1 (pp. 9–11), Ch. 3 (pp. 55–56), Ch. 5 (pp. 102–105); Hahnel & Albert (1990, Chs. 6–7). |

## 2. Organizational & Actor Topology Requirements

| ID | Requirement Statement | Classification | Source Citation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **REQ-ACT-01** | **Individuals and Living Units / Households:** Must be able to formulate personal/household consumption requests, submit work proposals in early rounds, apply for special-need allowances, save/borrow across years, and vote in council deliberations. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 3 (p. 41), Ch. 4 (pp. 63–69); LF Chs. 4, 8. |
| **REQ-ACT-02** | **Nested Consumer Councils and Federations:** Geographic hierarchy of Neighborhood Councils $\to$ Ward Councils $\to$ City/County Councils $\to$ State/Regional Federations $\to$ National/Coalition Federations, each requesting collective goods matched to its spatial scale and aggregating approved member requests. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 3 (pp. 40–42), Ch. 4 (pp. 62–64), Ch. 5 (pp. 81–82); LF Chs. 3, 4, 8. |
| **REQ-ACT-03** | **Worker Councils and Dual Federations (Industry & Regional):** Democratic workplace councils (with internal work teams/divisions) federated both *by industry* (to manage sector capacity, inter-firm scale economies, R&D, and $SB/SC$ benchmarking) and *geographically by region* (to manage local production externalities and regional infrastructure). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 2 (pp. 23–25), Ch. 4 (p. 62), Ch. 5 (pp. 88, 96–97); LF Chs. 1, 2, 7. |
| **REQ-ACT-04** | **Six Functional Facilitation Boards:** Specialized informational and coordination bodies (whose workers also hold BJCs): (1) Iteration Facilitation Boards (IFBs), (2) Consumption Facilitation Boards (CFBs), (3) Production Facilitation Boards (PFBs), (4) Employment Facilitation Boards (EFBs), (5) Household Facilitation Boards (HFBs), and (6) Updating Facilitation Boards (UFBs). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | LF Ch. 6 (`lf6_1.htm`), Ch. 9 (`lf9_2.htm`); PEPE Ch. 4 (p. 62). |
| **REQ-ACT-05** | **Communities of Affected Parties (CAPs) & Environmental Courts:** Ecological collectives of citizens residing in an airshed/watershed affected by specific pollutants, participating in the Pollution Demand-Revealing Mechanism (PDRM), backed by Environmental Courts to resolve boundary/classification disputes. | **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | DEP Ch. 7 (pp. 138–149); PEP User Story Map (2023); Sandström (2023). |
| **REQ-ACT-06** | **Stand-Alone Capital Asset Accounting Entities & Society Account:** Accounting entities that hold social ownership records of productive capital sets, charge user-right fees ($P_k$) to WCs, and clear macro-income and investment flows through a Society Account. | **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | Sandström (2020, Chs. 3–5; 2023). |
| **REQ-ACT-07** | **Sybil-Resistant, Non-Plutocratic Democratic Membership:** Protocol must bind human participants to council memberships and role delegations via community attestation and revocable credentials—without equating token holdings or computational power to voting weight. | **[TECH-EXTENSION]** | Derived from PEPE Ch. 2 (p. 23: "one worker, one vote") and Ch. 3 (p. 41: "one person, one vote"). |

## 3. Information & Data Model Requirements

| ID | Requirement Statement | Classification | Source Citation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **REQ-DATA-01** | **Five Core Priced Economic Categories (+ Pollutants):** The planning state must track supply, demand, surplus/deficit, and indicative prices for: (1) Private consumption goods, (2) Public/collective goods, (3) Intermediate produced inputs, (4) Non-producible natural resources & ecological sinks ($R$), (5) Labor categories ($L$), plus (6) Capital asset stocks ($K$) and (7) Pollutant emissions ($Z$). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 5 (pp. 78–88), Ch. 6 (pp. 110–113); DEP Ch. 7 (pp. 139–156), Ch. 9 (p. 176); Szczepanczyk (2023, Sec. 4). |
| **REQ-DATA-02** | **Coarse Product Taxonomy for Planning vs. Granular Fulfillment:** Annual planning aggregates interchangeable goods into coarse resource-equivalent categories; granular attributes (size, color, style) are statistically extrapolated during planning and resolved at point-of-consumption during the year. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (p. 64); LF Ch. 6 (`lf6_2.htm`); DEP Ch. 8 ("The size 6 purple women's high-heeled shoe with a yellow toe problem," pp. 163–167); Sandström (2025). |
| **REQ-DATA-03** | **Public Books of Technological Blueprints (Input-Output Recipes):** Unlike Debreuvian private-information models or patent monopolies, technical production relations $[A, K, R, L]$ and unit/industry input-output matrices $M_j$ are publicly shared across the economy, eliminating the capitalist conflict between innovation incentives (patents/secrecy) and static diffusion efficiency. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 5 (p. 84: "certainly the first thing a participatory economy would do is publish all its books of blueprints!"), Ch. 6 (pp. 111–113); PLAC Ch. 17 ("Meritocracy / Innovation"). |
| **REQ-DATA-04** | **Qualitative Information Lists (`Lists`) Paired with Quantitative Vectors:** Every council proposal vector must support structured qualitative annotations describing work conditions, health/safety impacts, and special consumption justifications, with hierarchical summarization by facilitation boards and drill-down access to raw council statements. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (pp. 61–62, 65), Ch. 6 (pp. 110–112); LF Ch. 6 (`lf6_2.htm`), Ch. 9 (`lf9_2.htm`). |
| **REQ-DATA-05** | **Privacy-Preserving Individual Consumption Within Warranted Budget:** While aggregate neighborhood demand vectors and above-average exception requests are visible to the council, an individual's itemized private consumption bundle within their warranted effort-rated budget is submitted anonymously, cannot be vetoed, and must be protected from neighbor snooping ("busybody economy" protection). | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** + **[TECH-EXTENSION]** | PEPE Ch. 3 (p. 41), Ch. 5 (p. 80); LF Ch. 3 (`lf3_2.htm`); PLAC Ch. 12, Ch. 18 ("A Busybody Economy?"); Dapprich (2024); extended via cryptographic commitments/zero-knowledge budget proofs. |

## 4. Functional & Algorithmic Lifecycle Requirements

| ID | Requirement Statement | Classification | Source Citation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **REQ-PLAN-01** | **Multi-Horizon Planning Sequencing:** Long-run development plans (education $l^*$, environment $r^*$, strategic trade) and aggregate investment plans establish fixed resource/capacity bounds *before* the annual participatory planning procedure commences. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | LF Ch. 9 (`lf9_1.htm`); DEP Chs. 11–15 (pp. 215–292). |
| **REQ-PLAN-02** | **Collective-Before-Personal Consumption Ordering:** National, state, county/city, and ward collective consumption proposals are formulated and voted on prior to (or in the earliest phases of) personal consumption planning so households know their collective tax/deduction share. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | LF Ch. 3 (`lf3_2.htm`), Ch. 8 (`lf8_1.htm`); PEPE Ch. 3 (p. 41). |
| **REQ-PLAN-03** | **Iterative Proposal-Aggregation-Pricing Cycle:** In each iteration $t$: (a) CCs submit private + collective demand vectors; (b) WCs submit output supply + input/resource/labor/capital/emission demand vectors; (c) IFBs sum global supply and demand, compute excess demand ratios, and update indicative prices $P^{(t+1)}$ via a publicly auditable rule; (d) councils revise proposals until all excess demands fall within threshold $\tau$ (e.g., 3%–5%) plus planned slack $SL^E$. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (pp. 63–71), Ch. 5 (pp. 89–95), Ch. 6 (p. 113); DEP Ch. 7 (pp. 130–158), Ch. 9 (pp. 176–185). |
| **REQ-PLAN-04** | **Enforcement of Equity & Efficiency Constraints:** (a) Consumer Council constraint: $P \cdot y(h) \le B(h)$ (where $B(h)$ is warranted income from effort ratings, benefits, CAP credits, and approved borrowing/needs); (b) Worker Council constraint: $SB(j) / SC(j) \ge 1$ (social benefit of outputs at least equals social cost of produced inputs, resources, labor endowments, capital user-rights, and emissions), with average effort ratings capped by $SB(j)/SC(j)$. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[INTERPRETATION-LATER]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (p. 66), Ch. 5 (pp. 80, 86–88); DEP Ch. 6 (p. 117), Ch. 7 (pp. 130–133, 151–156). |
| **REQ-PLAN-05** | **Convergence-Hastening Rules & Late-Round Plan Synthesis:** Support optional mid-round damping constraints (capping demand increases on excess-demand goods or supply cuts) and late-round IFB extrapolation of ~5 feasible macro-plan options for council voting, while preserving councils' right to reject IFB bundles and continue iterating. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (pp. 70–71); LF Ch. 9 (`lf9_2.htm`). |
| **REQ-PLAN-06** | **Intra-Year Execution Tracking & Hierarchical Variance Absorption:** Track real-time drawdowns and deliveries against the approved annual plan; absorb canceling consumer demand shifts locally within neighborhood/ward/city federations; trigger UFB-mediated renegotiation with industry federations and WCs only when net regional/aggregate demand shifts exceed planned slack buffers. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** | PEPE Ch. 4 (pp. 66–67); LF Ch. 6 (`lf6_1.htm`), Ch. 8; DEP Ch. 8 (pp. 167–170). |
| **REQ-PLAN-07** | **Verifiable Deterministic Aggregation and Price Calculation:** Because IFBs must not wield arbitrary central-planning authority, all aggregation and price-update computations must be deterministically reproducible and cryptographically verifiable by any council node. | **[ORIGINAL-1991]** + **[TECH-EXTENSION]** | LF Ch. 9 (`lf9_2.htm`: "supervision and checked by anyone who wished to"); extended via Merkle commitments and deterministic WASM/state transitions. |

## 5. Unresolved Questions Requiring Economic or Institutional Research

1. **[UNRESOLVED-01] Convergence Under Genuine Non-Convexities (Fixed Setup Costs & Discrete Scale Economies):**
   - Albert & Hahnel (1991a, Ch. 5, pp. 94–95) explicitly acknowledge that endogenous preferences and real industrial technologies violate standard convexity assumptions. While Hahnel et al. (2021, Ch. 9) tested Cobb-Douglas exponents summing to $>1$ (`toothaches?`), our code audit of `pequod-cljs` (`csvgen.clj`) proves that the simulation retained a strictly convex disutility-of-effort exponent $k$ that prevented true non-convex corner jumps. How the iterative protocol converges when worker councils face discrete lumpy capacities, minimum efficient scale, or multi-output joint production remains an open algorithmic research problem.
2. **[UNRESOLVED-02] Coupled Endogenous Income Feedback During Planning Iterations:**
   - In theoretical PE/FMPE, consumer budgets depend on worker effort ratings and total social output, which change across iterations. However, `pequod-cljs` fixed consumer income exogenously during iterations (`util.cljc` line 48). Coupling dynamic income updates into the tâtonnement loop introduces cross-market income effects (potentially violating gross substitutability) whose stability must be tested in simulation.
3. **[UNRESOLVED-03] Biophysical Incommensurability vs. PDRM Monetary Compensation:**
   - As John O'Neill (2024) demonstrates, Hahnel's PDRM prices local pollutants via CAP willingness-to-accept consumption compensation, which fails when ecological thresholds are non-substitutable, cumulative, or affect non-human ecosystems and future generations. How hard biophysical caps ($r^*, E^{\max}$) from long-run environmental planning interact mathematically with annual shadow prices requires formal dual-constraint integration.
4. **[UNRESOLVED-04] Cognitive Burden, Participation Fatigue, and Default Automation Boundaries:**
   - Critics (Schweickart 1993; Ackerman 2014) highlight the time burden of annual household consumption proposals and multi-level council meetings. Determining how much proposal drafting can be delegated to local forecasting/optimization tools without eroding genuine democratic self-management requires human-in-the-loop experimental testing.
5. **[UNRESOLVED-05] Workplace Effort-Rating Dynamics and Social Trust:**
   - Whether peer-determined effort ratings (even when capped at council average $100 \times SB/SC$) foster solidarity or induce interpersonal conflict, surveillance anxiety (O'Neill 2024), or coarse egalitarian flattening (every council simply voting equal 100 ratings for all members) cannot be resolved by computer simulation and requires empirical study in real worker cooperatives.
6. **[UNRESOLVED-06] Federation Gatekeeping and Appellate Power:**
   - Industry federations hold the power to accredit new worker councils, adjudicate whether a council with $SB/SC < 1$ is "lazy" or disadvantaged by accounting artifacts, and dissolve persistently underperforming councils (PEPE Ch. 4, p. 70; Kuch 2024). Institutional rules preventing incumbent councils in an industry federation from cartelizing or blocking disruptive innovations remain underspecified.
