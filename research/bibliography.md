## 3. Economic Calculation, Mechanism Design, and Critical Literature

9. **Arrow, Kenneth J., Leonid Hurwicz, and Hirofumi Uzawa (1958).** *Studies in Linear and Nonlinear Programming*. Stanford, CA: Stanford University Press.
   - **Role:** Foundational saddle-point convergence theorems for gradient allocation procedures cited in Albert & Hahnel (1991a, Ch. 5, p. 105 n.3).

10. **Heal, Geoffrey M. (1973).** *The Theory of Economic Planning*. Amsterdam: North-Holland Publishing Co. ISBN 978-0-7204-3073-8.
    - **Role:** Chapter 4 provides the formal convergence analysis of the Lange-Arrow-Hurwicz price-guided iterative procedure adapted in FMPE (Albert & Hahnel 1991a, Ch. 5, p. 93).

11. **Henry, Claude (1972).** "Differential Equations with Discontinuous Right-Hand Sides in Mathematical Economics." *Journal of Economic Theory* 4(3): 545–551. [https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(72)90138-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(72)90138-X)
    - **Role:** Extends Lyapunov stability to non-negativity boundary projections in gradient planning procedures (cited in Albert & Hahnel 1991a, p. 105 n.4).

12. **Hurwicz, Leonid (1973).** "The Design of Mechanisms for Resource Allocation." *American Economic Review* 63(2): 1–30.
    - **Role:** Establishes incentive compatibility, informational decentralization, and the impossibility of dominant-strategy Pareto-optimal budget-balanced mechanisms in classical environments.

13. **Groves, Theodore, and John Ledyard (1977).** "Optimal Allocation of Public Goods: A Solution to the 'Free Rider' Problem." *Econometrica* 45(4): 783–809. [https://doi.org/10.2307/1912672](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912672)
    - **Role:** Demand-revealing public goods mechanism evaluated in Albert & Hahnel (1991a, Ch. 3 pp. 43–44, Ch. 5 pp. 96, 100).

14. **Hayek, Friedrich A. (1945).** "The Use of Knowledge in Society." *American Economic Review* 35(4): 519–530.
    - **Role:** Classic statement of the dispersed and tacit knowledge problem in economic coordination.

15. **Lange, Oskar, and Fred M. Taylor (1938/1964).** *On the Economic Theory of Socialism*, ed. Benjamin E. Lippincott. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press / New York: Monthly Review Press.
    - **Role:** Trial-and-error shadow price adjustment model in the socialist calculation debate.

16. **Nove, Alec (1983).** *The Economics of Feasible Socialism*. London: George Allen & Unwin. ISBN 978-0-04-335049-2.
    - **Role:** Formulates the "no third way" thesis between markets and hierarchical command planning due to scale and complexity (pp. ix–x, 44).

17. **Cockshott, W. Paul, and Allin Cottrell (1993).** *Towards a New Socialism*. Nottingham: Spokesman Books.
    - **URL:** [https://users.wfu.edu/cottrell/socialism_book/](https://users.wfu.edu/cottrell/socialism_book/)
    - **Role:** Primary cybernetic central-planning alternative using direct linear programming and labor-time calculation; critiques Parecon's iterative council bargaining.

18. **Devine, Pat (1988).** *Democracy and Economic Planning: The Political Economy of a Self-Governing Society*. Boulder, CO: Westview Press.
    - **Role:** Model of "negotiated coordination" contrasted with Parecon in Albert & Hahnel (1991a, p. 71 n.1) and Hahnel (2021, Appendix, pp. 323–331).

19. **Schweickart, David (1993).** *Against Capitalism*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. [https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511558993](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511558993)
    - **Role:** Market-socialist ("Economic Democracy") critique of Parecon's consumption-list burden, effort rating, and IFB macro-plan voting.

20. **Ackerman, Seth (2014).** "Horizontalism and Idealism in Socialist Imagination: An Appraisal of the Participatory Economy." *Science & Society* 78(2): 207–239. [https://doi.org/10.1521/siso.2014.78.2.207](https://doi.org/10.1521/siso.2014.78.2.207)
    - **Role:** Critiques Parecon's Walrasian equilibrium assumptions and institutional rigidity.

21. **Hahnel, Robin, and Erik Olin Wright (2016).** *Alternatives to Capitalism: Proposals for a Democratic Economy*. London: Verso Books. ISBN 978-1-78478-504-8.
    - **Role:** Debate between comprehensive participatory planning (Hahnel) and pluralist institutional ecosystems combining democratic planning, social economy, and regulated markets (Wright).

22. **O'Neill, John (2024).** "Incommensurability, Environment and Planning: A Response to Hahnel's Reply" and "Socialism, Ecology and Economic Calculation." *Erasmus Journal for Philosophy and Economics* 17(2): 29–58. [https://ejpe.org/journal/issue/download/35/67](https://ejpe.org/journal/issue/download/35/67)
    - **Access Status:** Full special issue PDF accessed and analyzed on November 4, 2026. Critiques shadow-price commensurability of ecological boundaries and peer effort surveillance.

23. **Kuch, Hannes (2024).** "Decentralization, Federations, and Authority in a Participatory Economy." *Erasmus Journal for Philosophy and Economics* 17(2). [https://ejpe.org/journal/issue/download/35/67](https://ejpe.org/journal/issue/download/35/67)
    - **Access Status:** Accessed November 4, 2026. Analyzes centralization pressures in industry and consumer federations.

24. **Dapprich, Jan Philipp (2024).** "Democratic Theory and Economic Planning: A Critique of Participatory Economics." *Erasmus Journal for Philosophy and Economics* 17(2). [https://ejpe.org/journal/article/view/867](https://ejpe.org/journal/article/view/867)
    - **Access Status:** Accessed November 4, 2026. Argues democratic legitimacy applies to general planning rules rather than collective vetoes over concrete individual plans.

## 4. Historical and Institutional Precedents

25. **Dolgoff, Sam, ed. (1974).** *The Anarchist Collectives: Workers' Self-Management in the Spanish Revolution, 1936–1939*. New York: Free Life Editions / Montreal: Black Rose Books.
    - **Online Full Text:** [https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/sam-dolgoff-editor-the-anarchist-collectives](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/sam-dolgoff-editor-the-anarchist-collectives)
    - **Access Status:** Full text accessed and analyzed on November 4, 2026; includes eyewitness studies by Gaston Leval, Augustin Souchy, and José Peirats.

26. **Leval, Gaston (1975).** *Collectives in the Spanish Revolution*, trans. Vernon Richards. London: Freedom Press.
    - **Role:** Primary empirical source on industrial and agrarian collectives in Catalonia, Aragon, and Valencia, including the Council of Aragon and regional equalization funds.

27. **Mintz, Frank (2013 [1970/1977]).** *Anarchism and Workers' Self-Management in Revolutionary Spain*, trans. Paul Sharkey. Oakland, CA: AK Press. ISBN 978-1-84935-078-5.
    - **Role:** Quantitative demographic and economic assessment of Spanish collectivization (~1.84M–2.44M participants) and analysis of inter-collective inequalities and wartime accounting failures.

28. **Marx, Karl (1871).** *The Civil War in France*. London: General Council of the International Workingmen's Association.
    - **Role:** Primary political analysis of the Paris Commune's revocable delegates, workman's wage cap, and proposed national federation of communes.

29. **Merriman, John (2014).** *Massacre: The Life and Death of the Paris Commune of 1871*. New York: Basic Books. ISBN 978-0-465-02089-8.
    - **Role:** Historical analysis distinguishing the Commune's actual 72-day administrative enactments (Labor Commission under Léo Frankel, abandoned workshop decrees, pawnshop/rent reforms) from unimplemented federalist blueprints.

30. **Hansen, Mogens Herman (1991).** *The Athenian Democracy in the Age of Demosthenes: Structure, Principles, and Ideology*. Oxford: Blackwell / Norman: University of Oklahoma Press. ISBN 978-0-8061-3143-6.
    - **Role:** Definitive institutional reference on Athenian sortition (*kleroterion*), *Boule* of 500, *Ekklesia*, *dokimasia*, *euthynai*, *graphe paranomon*, and demographic exclusion of women, metics, and enslaved persons.

31. **Freeman, Jo (1972).** "The Tyranny of Structurelessness." *The Second Wave* 2(1) / *Berkeley Journal of Sociology* 17: 151–164.
    - **Role:** Foundational institutional critique of informal elites and unaccountable leadership in formally horizontal movements.

32. **Graeber, David (2013).** *The Democracy Project: A History, a Crisis, a Movement*. New York: Spiegel & Grau. ISBN 978-0-8129-9356-1.
    - **Role:** Primary participant-ethnographic account of Occupy Wall Street's General Assemblies, spokescouncils, modified consensus, and working-group coordination bottlenecks.

33. **Ostrom, Elinor (1990).** *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. [https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763)
    - **Role:** Establishes the eight institutional design principles for enduring self-governed common-pool resources (defined boundaries, proportional equivalence, collective-choice arrangements, accountable monitoring, graduated sanctions, low-cost conflict resolution, nested enterprises).

34. **Whyte, William Foote, and Kathleen King Whyte (1991).** *Making Mondragón: The Growth and Dynamics of the Worker Cooperative Complex*, 2nd ed. Ithaca, NY: ILR Press.
    - **Role:** Empirical study of federated worker cooperatives, internal capital accounts, wage-solidarity ratios, and tensions with global market competition.
